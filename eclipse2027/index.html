Where to look

Face east‑southeast, look up

At maximum the Sun sits about 39° above the horizon toward the east‑southeast (azimuth ≈ 96°). That's a comfortable height — roughly halfway between the horizon and straight overhead.

  • ↗

    Pick an open eastern horizon

    Sea‑facing spots on Marbella's coast are ideal — nothing tall to the ESE.

  • ◷

    Be in place by 10:30

    The last minutes before totality change fast — light, temperature, shadows.

  • △

    Watch the ground too

    Shadow bands may ripple across pale surfaces seconds before and after totality.

During those 3 minutes

The things nobody photographs well

  • 01

    The diamond ring

    The final sliver of photosphere blazes through a lunar valley — one brilliant point on a thin ring. It bookends totality at both ends.

  • 02

    Baily's beads

    Sunlight streams through gaps between lunar mountains, breaking the crescent into a string of glittering beads.

  • 03

    The corona

    The Sun's million‑degree outer atmosphere — pearly white streamers you can only ever see with the disc fully hidden. No two eclipses look alike.

  • 04

    The chromosphere

    A thin scarlet rim and occasional pink prominences flare at the Moon's edge for a few seconds at each contact.

  • 05

    A 360° sunset

    You're standing in a shadow ~100 km wide. Beyond its edge the sky still glows, so sunset colours wrap the entire horizon.

  • 06

    The temperature drops

    Expect a sudden 5–8 °C fall and a hush — birds roost, cicadas quieten, the sea breeze shifts.

  • 07

    Planets and stars at midday

    Venus and bright stars pop out around the blackened Sun in the middle of a summer morning.

Read this first

Your eyes have one job

  • ✓

    Certified filters for every partial phase

    Use ISO 12312‑2 eclipse glasses or solar filters from 09:41 until totality, and again the instant it ends.

  • ◐

    Naked eye only during totality

    For the 3m 21s the disc is fully covered — and only then — it is safe and spectacular to look directly.

  • ⚠

    The second diamond ring is the warning

    The moment a bright point returns, filters go back on. No cameras or binoculars on the Sun without solar filters.
